## The story in one breath

Rhinos burst out of thick fog head-on. They aren't angry; they can barely see. Their lookout bird, the little oxpecker who is their eyes, has fallen in the mud, and without her they are stampeding blind toward a cliff. So the pack does something no one would expect: it goes after the herd that nearly flattened it. The Leopard leaps from rhino to rhino to put the bird back on the leader's horn, one stride from the edge.

**What the viewer should feel, in order:** a jump scare → a laugh (the warthogs) → pity for the bird → alarm → the thrill of the chase → fear at the edge → joy at the leap → relief, then a grin.

**The lesson, never said out loud:** be kind to the ones who scared you. They might just be lost.

## The world

- **Place:** a wet highland at dawn. Rolling ground, tussock grass, dark rounded boulders, a few twisted trees that are only silhouettes. The ground is red-brown mud with standing water.
- **Fog:** for the first twelve seconds you can see about thirty metres. Things arrive as grey shapes and become solid only when they're close. The sun is a soft glowing disc.
- **Palette in the fog:** fog `#e9edf0`; sun glow `#ffd9a8`; mud `#6e5340` with wet highlights `#b9a58c`; grass `#6f8a5a`. Cool grade `#bcd3e6` at about 0.10.
- **Palette after 17.4 s:** the sun burns the fog off. The grade warms to `#ffc98a`, and a green valley opens far below the cliff, with a river of mist in it and a dark storm building on the far side.
- **The look:** grey shapes, then colour. The episode starts almost monochrome and ends in full morning light. That change is the reward.

## The three shots that must land

1. **The hook (0.0–1.0 s).** Fog hides things, and the first second has to show a threat. Solve it with the fog itself: from the very first frame a dark mass is pushing through the white ahead, and at 0.4 s it becomes horns. The frame at 0.9 s is the thumbnail: a rhino's horn and head bursting out of white, mud flying, the pack in front.
2. **The chase turned round (9.0–12.4 s).** The pack is now running *after* the danger. The pack can't actually turn in this engine, so stage it: a colour wipe at 9.0 s, and after it the herd is in front, seen from behind. The viewer must understand the reversal without a word, so make the framing the mirror of the hook: at the hook horns came at us; now we see tails and the cats gaining.
3. **The leap chain (13.8–16.2 s).** Three leaps along three moving backs, side-on, in slow motion, with the cliff edge coming. Each landing is a thump in the sound. Then the smallest character in the episode stops two tons of rhino with four words. Keep the edge in frame the whole time so the distance closing is always visible.

## Music and sound

- Minor and tense, with a driving ostinato for the chase: start from `EPIC(39, 53, 128)`.
- Hits in `flashes`: the hook (about 0.45), the cliff (12.4) and the turn (16.2). The turn is the biggest; the near-silence before it sits under the bird's shout.
- Fog should sound close and muffled. After 17.4 s let the sound open up with the picture.

## What exists and what's new

**Reuse:** the rhino; a head-on herd (eps 31 and 34); a herd running ahead of the pack with `dir: 1`; `haze`; `slow`; the colour wipe (`slashes`); the boar as the base for the warthogs.

**New:**
- **Fog as distance.** Things fade in by how far away they are, and the fog can tear open and burn off over time. The `haze` and far-animal fogging in the realism layer are the place to start.
- **The cliff edge.** A drop, not a wall: ground that ends, with a valley far below that moves slowly behind it. The existing `cliff` rises; `gorge` is a gap crossed by a bridge.
- **The herd swinging aside at the edge** and then running along it.
- **The leap chain,** and **a cat riding an animal** for the rest of the episode.
- **The oxpecker sprite,** riding a cat's head and then perched on a horn, with bubbles anchored to her in both places.
- **Warthog details** on the boar.
- **A grade that changes over time,** cool to warm.

## Don't

- Don't make the rhinos mean. No red eyes, no snarling. They're scared.
- Don't add hyenas, a storm or any second danger. The cliff is where the same stampede is heading.
- Don't let any rhino go over the edge, even nearly. Only mud and stones fall.
- Don't lose the cats in the fog. Their rim light and the fog's brightness should keep them the clearest things in frame.
- Don't show the valley before 12.4 s. The reveal is the reward.
