## The story in one breath

A red dune as tall as a skyscraper collapses and pours down on the pack. The Black Panther picks up a fennec fox and her kit, whose den has just been buried. Her enormous ears can hear sand move before it falls, and she calls the next collapse before it happens. Then the ground itself starts sliding and there is nowhere left to run. She asks for silence, listens, and hears the one place that isn't moving. It's *up*. The pack turns and runs up the falling dune.

**What the viewer should feel, in order:** awe and shock → tenderness for the foxes → trust (her ears were right) → real fear when the ground goes → a hush → exhilaration on the climb → peace on the ridge.

**The lesson, never said out loud:** stop and listen. The small one hears what the fast ones miss.

The series' most-watched Short is *Avalanche*, a wall of snow. Cold snow scenes otherwise did badly. This is that avalanche in warm colours, so the warmth of the picture is part of the brief.

## The world

- **Place:** a sea of red dunes at sunset. The pack runs along the firm corridor at the foot of one giant dune, which stands on one side of the frame like a wall, its crest a sharp line against the sky.
- **Palette:** sky `#3b2f6e` → `#e0703c` → `#ffc27a`. Sand in sunlight `#e8843c`; sand in shadow `#8a3a2a`; highlights `#ffd9a0`. Dust `rgb(236,160,100)`. Grade `#ff7a2a` at about 0.18.
- **The look:** two tones. Every dune has a burning orange face and a plum shadow face, with a knife-edge between them. Long shadows stretch from the cats.
- **The ridge:** along the top of the giant dune runs a spine of dark rock (`#3a2a2a`). It is barely visible until the payoff. It's the only thing in the landscape that isn't sand.
- **Start from:** a new biome on the `desert` base. Put "dune" and "desert" in its name so it gets the wind bed.

## The three shots that must land

1. **The hook (0.4–1.6 s).** Scale is everything. The dune must be so tall that the cats are tiny at its foot, and the collapse must start at the crest and take a moment to arrive, so the viewer sees what's coming before it comes. The frame at 0.9 s is the thumbnail: an orange wall of sand pouring down, the pack small in front. Make it read as sand, with grain, streaks and a dust plume, not as fog or snow.
2. **The listening (13.8–15.8 s).** After twelve seconds of roar, cut the music and nearly everything else. Two bubbles, her ears, the hiss of sand. The earlier beat at 10.2–10.9 is what makes this work: the viewer has already seen her ears be right once, so they believe her when she says the impossible thing. Don't cut that setup.
3. **The climb (15.9–17.8 s).** Running *up* a collapsing dune is the image of the episode. Sell the steepness with the camera, not with new cat animation: tilt and roll the view so the slope is a diagonal, stream the sand down past the cats in the opposite direction, and move the pack toward the crest. Then the dark rock, the leap, and the whole face falling away behind them.

## What exists and what's new

**Reuse:** the chasing `wall` (eps 9 and 23 used it for snow); the oryx as a `dir: 1` herd (ep 33); riders on the lead cat and `back: true` bubbles (the foxes ride the Black Panther, so this works as it is); `sand` and `haze` (ep 33); a `cliff` on one side as the starting point for the dune face (ep 14).

**New:**
- **A sand style for the wall:** lobes, streaks, plume, in the two-tone palette.
- **The giant dune** as a slope on one side with a crest line, a crack that travels along it, and the rock spine.
- **The second collapse** at 10.9, on the opposite side from the first.
- **Sliding ground:** ripples moving across the ground while the pack drifts sideways.
- **The climb:** the tilted camera, sand streaming down, the pack's shift toward the crest, and the group leap onto rock.
- **The ridge run:** a narrow path with falling dunes on both sides.
- **A fennec sprite** in two sizes, with ears that can swivel separately.
- **A bubble anchored to a point the pack is leaving behind** (the kit at the cave) that stays on screen long enough to read.

## Don't

- Don't make it look cold. No blue, no white, no snow-like softness.
- Don't add a sandstorm, a scorpion or any second danger. The only threat is the falling sand.
- Don't let the foxes' rescue stop the pack. They jump on at a run and jump off at a run.
- Don't put music under 13.8–15.8 s.
- Don't let any cat be buried or tumble. They slip; they never fall.
